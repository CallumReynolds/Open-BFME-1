Normalizing numeric-address constants into literals changes the mask but leaves the same 522-byte instruction structure. Correcting that order and using typed line-array access removes the extra LEA at +0x148 and yields 529 bytes with 26 stack-offset differences. The initial hypothesis was that the loop access expression and integer arithmetic, rather than an irreducible register allocation choice, explained the structural residue. Unchanged instruction output would have refuted it. Trials 02 and 03 changed the exact implicated instructions, and trials 08 and 24 resolved the remaining lifetime and aggregate storage differences. Position-only aggregation, measured-size aggregation, a mutated position accumulator, lexical scopes around rounding or frame calculation, combined fraction spelling and long rounding destinations did not produce a better body.
