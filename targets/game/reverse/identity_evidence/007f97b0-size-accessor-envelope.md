## Retry and refutation

Earlier records tested allocator and string-helper visibility, ordinary compiler switches, and a volatility search. This retry tested whether source-level accessors and complete construction or copying lifetimes explain the post-allocation scheduling and later envelope residue. The allocation accessor hypothesis is supported by the controlled measurements in 28-size-accessor-only.log, 29-size-accessor-allocation-only.log and 30-size-accessor-string-only.log. Removing unused accessors leaves the improvement intact. Restricting the accessor to the allocation call preserves it; restricting it to the string lookup loses it. Returning the same field by reference or pointer also loses it (107-size-reference-return.log and 108-size-pointer-return.log). An identical out-of-class inline value accessor retains it (109-size-outofline-inline-control.log). The value-returning call boundary is supported; a specific definition site is not required here.

The remaining construction and copy hypotheses did not improve the retained candidate. A future reopening needs a demonstrated change in the envelope instructions beginning at +0x130, or independently recovered source structure that differs from the retained experiments. The evidence does not prove that assembly is necessary.
